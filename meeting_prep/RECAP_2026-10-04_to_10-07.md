# Recap: 2026-10-04 to 10-07

Picks up after the 10-03 decision to switch the objective to **tardiness** and the action space to the **H15** heads
(job {SRT, SPT, MDD, EDD, ATC} x machine {ECT, TECT, SRWT}). Every item below has a write-up in `results/<name>/README.md`.

## TL;DR

1. **The headroom is real.** A switching oracle beats the best fixed pair by about 9-13% on tardiness, on the twin
   (10.8%, 40 seeds) and in Unity (8.8-12.8% median, 8 seeds per setting).
2. **RL has not captured it yet.**
   - Per-decision PPO on the twin: 11-17% *worse* than MDD-TECT (it mixed rules decision by decision).
   - Slot actions (one rule pair per 15 min): parity with MDD-TECT, small wins on most instances and large losses
     (up to +30%) on a few.
   - An action prior on MDD-TECT: removes the losses but collapses to MDD-TECT (+0.0%).
3. **Diagnosis.**
   - What isn't the problem: missing congestion or history inputs, network size, a bad corner of the scenario
     generator.
   - The right choice depends on random arrivals that the state barely predicts: AUC <= 0.70 even with oracle
     labels.
   - Behavior cloning of the oracle beats MDD-TECT by 1-2.6% with no big losses. That is the best policy so far, and
     a better starting point for RL.
4. **Episode length matters, as the advisor suggested.** 6 h episodes (~300 jobs) measure a floor that is still
   warming up. The "each episode has its own best rule" effect mostly disappears by 96-200 h (4,500-9,400 jobs), and
   the best fixed rule changes (MDD-TECT -> ATC-TECT). Within long episodes, the local switching opportunity remains.

## 1. Setting up the tardiness problem (10-03/04)

- **Load calibration** (`dev-load-calib`): the old load made 6 h episodes grow without bound. Utilization 0.6-1.2
  levels off after ~3 h. The training regime **B2** mixes normal, surge (1.0-1.6) and short-fleet (2 AGVs) blocks of
  1.5 h. On the twin, transport only binds at 2 AGVs (Unity: ~4).
- **Twin headroom** (`rq2-twin-fleet`): 6 h episodes give 8.2% median in-episode headroom vs 2.4% for 1.5 h. Regime and
  fleet blocks add variety, not extra headroom.
- **Unity headroom:**
  - `rq2-oracle-due` (c = 2): 3.4% median
  - `rq2-oracle-due-mix` (c x load mix): 10.5% median over 19 seeds (one timed out)
  - `rq2-oracle-due-long`: blocks-u 12.8% mean / 8.8% median, long-u 10.8% / 9.7%, with 30-min slots
- **Fixed-pair baseline** (`base-due-c2`): MDD-TECT is the best fixed rule on tardiness; FIFO and PTWINQ never win.
- **Compute** (`twin-compute`, RIT cluster): PPO on the twin runs at **380 steps/s** vs ~10 on Unity (40x). A 3M-step
  run is ~2 h on the twin vs 3-5 days on Unity, so training moved to the twin.

## 4. Why RL struggles: diagnostics (10-06/07)

| check | question | answer |
|---|---|---|
| `eval-due-twin-slot-long/workup` (40 + 200 instances) | Is there a scenario property where the policies lose? | No. Nothing separates bad from good instances (classifier AUC 0.35-0.56). Losses follow "ATC was the wrong family here". |
| `dev-congestion-signal` | Would a transport-backlog scalar help (RQ on congestion in the observation)? | Not on the twin: obs v3 already carries it (WIP, idle-AGV flag, AGVs on duty). Adding it: AUC change ±0.01. Unity, with physical congestion, is untested. |
| `dev-history-signal` | Does memory (recurrence or attention over past slots) help? | No. Lagged observations add nothing; AUC -0.02 to -0.04. |
| `dev-oracle-bc` | Can the network imitate the oracle at all? | It memorizes the training instances (78%) but gets 21% on new ones, the same as a scalars-only MLP. Not a capacity problem. As a policy it still beats MDD-TECT (-1 to -2.6%). |
| `rq2-oracle-slotlen` (cluster) | Does the decision interval matter? | The oracle's 10.8% = ~4.8 pts picking the right fixed pair per instance + ~6 pts switching. 1 h slots keep 82% with a quarter of the decisions. |
| `base-eplen` (cluster, 480 tasks) | Are 6 h episodes too short? | Yes, see section 5. |

## 5. Episode length (`base-eplen`; all 15 fixed pairs, 40 seeds, windows 6 / 24 / 96 / 200 h)

| B2, 1.5 h blocks | 6 h | 24 h | 96 h | 200 h |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| jobs out | 271 | 1,129 | 4,541 | 9,419 |
| hindsight gain of picking each instance's best fixed pair | 7.9% | 5.4% | 1.5% | 0.6% |
| instances whose best pair is the overall best | 20% | 28% | 53% | 75% |
| best pair on average | MDD-TECT | MDD-TECT | ATC-TECT | ATC-TECT |
| tardiness per job, MDD-TECT (s) | 604 | 1,039 | 1,303 | 1,326 |

- **6 h episodes sit in the warm-up transient.** Tardiness per 6 h chunk roughly doubles over the first day, then
  levels off. Every oracle and RL number above was measured on that transient.
- **The per-episode preference was mostly small-sample noise.** Inside long episodes, each 6 h chunk still has its own
  best pair (8% hindsight gain on B2; 19% with 6 h regime blocks), so switching remains worthwhile.
- **Longer regime blocks** (6 h) make the differences between chunks larger and more persistent: a better-posed
  switching problem.

## 6. For discussion / next steps (proposed, not started)

1. **Move training and evaluation to long or continuing episodes at steady state**, scored per 6 h chunk. Also re-pick
   the default rule there (ATC-TECT on B2 with 1.5 h blocks).
2. **Start RL from the cloned oracle policy**, with the KL anchor to the cloned policy instead of to one fixed pair.
3. **More oracle data on the cluster** (~200 instances / 4 h). Data, not network size, limits imitation.
4. **Longer slots (30-60 min) and longer regime blocks** to strengthen the learning signal.
5. **Congestion in the observation (RQ):** the twin has no zone blocking, so test it in Unity, where congestion is
   physical. The twin result alone does not answer the RQ.
6. **Open decision:** the regime and backend for the final Unity training (`train-tardiness`). blocks-u mirrors B2
   for twin pre-training (twin-transfer).
